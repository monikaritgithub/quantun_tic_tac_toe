Rules of Quantum Tic-Tac-Toe

Invented by physicist Dr. Allan Goff (Novatia Labs, American Journal of Physics, 2006), Quantum Tic-Tac-Toe models quantum mechanics using a pure, discrete graph without requiring linear algebra.

1. Superposition (Two Spooky Marks Per Turn)

Players alternate turns (Player X goes first with odd moves 1, 3, 5...; Player O plays even moves 2, 4, 6...). Instead of placing one permanent piece, each player places two "spooky" marks in two different open squares (e.g., Move 1 places X1 in Square 1 and Square 5). This represents a quantum state in equal superposition.

2. Entanglement (Shared Squares)

Any square can hold multiple spooky marks from different turns. When a new spooky mark shares a square with a previous move, the two moves become entangled, forming a network of quantum dependencies.

3. Cyclic Entanglement (Measurement Trigger)

As the board fills, moves create connections across squares. The moment a player’s move forms a closed loop (cycle) in the entanglement network, the system reaches its critical measurement threshold. Normal play pauses and an immediate collapse is forced!

4. Opponent Chooses the Collapse

The player whose move created the cycle does not choose how it resolves. Instead, their opponent gets to select one of the two possible classical configurations (Configuration A or Configuration B) for the loop!

5. Cascading Domino Collapse

Resolving the cycle forces one mark in each cycle square to solidify into a permanent classical X or O. Any other moves entangled with those squares are forced down their dependent branches in a deterministic cascade. Squares that collapse become permanently closed classical tiles.

6. Winning & The Subscript Tiebreaker Rule

A player wins by achieving 3 classical marks in a row (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal). If a collapse causes both players to complete 3-in-a-row simultaneously, Allan Goff's Subscript Tiebreaker Rule applies:

  • Each winning line is evaluated by the maximum move subscript that created it.
  • The player with the smaller maximum subscript completed their line earlier in time and is awarded 1.0 point (Winner).
  • The opponent receives 0.5 points.
